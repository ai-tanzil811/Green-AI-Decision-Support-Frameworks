Multi-objective decision support Surrogate status: preliminary

Choose how to fine-tune an LLM before you spend the energy to find out.

GreenPEFT estimates accuracy, peak VRAM, energy, carbon and wall-clock time for every candidate PEFT configuration from pre-run metadata alone. It then filters against your hardware and accuracy constraints, extracts the Pareto frontier, and ranks what survives with the Green Efficiency Index — so a sweep becomes a shortlist.

02The problem

Trial and error is the most expensive way to learn what will not fit.

A fine-tuning sweep spends real memory, real wattage and real hours to discover facts that were largely determined the moment the configuration was written down.

01

Decisions are made by running everything.

The pilot grid in this repository attempted 60 runs across five PEFT strategies and four backbone scales. Nineteen ended in an out-of-memory error — close to a third of the attempts spent confirming that a configuration was never viable on the device.

02

The objectives genuinely conflict.

Full fine-tuning reached 0.9052 accuracy on the 0.5 B backbone but needed 11.13 GB and could not run at all above it. QLoRA completed every attempted cell and used as little as 2.76 GB, yet took the longest wall-clock time at each scale. No single row wins on all four axes.

03

Constraints can be answered before the run.

Peak VRAM, energy and runtime are largely a function of parameter count, adapter rank and quantisation width — all known in advance. GreenPEFT fits surrogates on that metadata and turns a budget into a ranked, inspectable shortlist in well under a second.

Worked example

LISA fits a 1.5 B backbone inside 16 GB. Full fine-tuning does not.

Full fine-tuning completed all three seeds at 0.5 B, peaking at 11.13 GB, then went out of memory on every attempt at 1.1 B, 1.5 B and 3.0 B — nine failed runs and a 0.75 OOM rate for the strategy. LISA, which samples and updates a subset of layers per step, completed the 1.5 B cell at 15.21 GB peak VRAM and 0.9381 accuracy in 140.95 s. That is the trade the framework exists to surface: the strategy that is not the most accurate in the abstract can be the only one that clears the hardware constraint you actually have.

05Green Efficiency Index

One score, four objectives, weights you choose.

GEI is a weighted sum of four min–max normalised objectives. The normalisation bounds come from the feasible set — options already ruled out should not stretch the scale for the ones still on the table.

Definition

GEI(c)=wacc·Ŝacc+wmem·Ŝmem+wC·ŜC+wT·ŜT

Ŝacc = (acc − accmin) ⁄ (accmax − accmin)
ŜM = 1 − (M − Mmin) ⁄ (Mmax − Mmin)  for M ∈ {mem, C, T}
Σ wᵢ = 1 · bounds from the feasible set · higher is better

06Model card · evidence boundary

What this model knows, and where it stops knowing it.

The surrogate's overall status is preliminary, taken from the weaker of two validation protocols. That label appears on the CLI banner, in the model metadata and here, because a shortlist is only useful if you know how far to trust it.

Out-of-sample validation
Empirical back-test
Measured envelope

What it is for

  • Planning an experiment before any GPU time is committed.
  • Generating a shortlist of two or three configurations worth measuring.
  • Screening out configurations that will not fit a known VRAM budget.
  • Comparing strategies under an explicit, stated weighting of objectives.

What it is not

  • It does not run fine-tuning. It recommends; you execute.
  • It does not guarantee the accuracy, energy, carbon or runtime you will observe.
  • The error bands are measured out-of-sample error, not calibrated prediction intervals.
  • It does not eliminate emissions — it reduces the compute spent discovering dead ends.

Where the evidence ends

  • All direct measurement is SST-2 classification on one NVIDIA Tesla T4, 15.6 GB usable.
  • Measured span: 0.5–3.0 B parameters, families qwen and llama, 14 of 20 (method, scale) cells.
  • Catalogue entries outside that span are extrapolations, labelled OUT_OF_RANGE_SCALE or UNSEEN_FAMILY and given LOW confidence.
  • Beyond 3.0 B the reported band is a lower bound: the extrapolation protocol never tested that far.

How to use it responsibly

  • Treat the top-ranked candidate as a hypothesis, then measure it.
  • Raise the evidence floor to measured when the stakes are high.
  • Record the real run with experiments/validate_recommendation.py.
  • Read the scope label before the number — it says which protocol's error applies.

Twelve of the 70 catalogue candidates produce physically impossible predictions: negative peak VRAM below 0.5 B, accuracy above 1.0 at 7 B. The Ridge surrogates are linear, so far outside the fitted range they extrapolate straight through those limits. Those candidates are dropped before scoring with a stated reason rather than silently ranked — one negative VRAM value would otherwise pass every budget and rescale the memory objective for everything else.
